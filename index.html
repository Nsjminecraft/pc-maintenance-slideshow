Safety Precautions

Essential safety measures before starting

5 Critical Safety Considerations

1ESD Protection

Wear an anti-static wrist strap connected to ground to prevent electrostatic discharge that can damage sensitive components.

2Power Disconnection

Ensure the PC is completely powered down and unplugged from the wall outlet. Press power button to discharge residual electricity.

3Proper Tool Usage

Use magnetic-tipped screwdrivers and appropriate tools. Never force components - if something doesn't fit, check alignment.

4Component Handling

Handle components by edges only. Never touch circuit boards, gold contacts, or pins. Place parts on anti-static mats.

5Workspace Organization

Maintain clean, well-lit workspace. Use labeled containers for screws. Keep cables organized and avoid tripping hazards.

Step 4: Disassembly Process

Systematic removal of components

Disassembly Order

  • 1Disconnect all power cables from components
  • 2Disconnect SATA/data cables
  • 3Remove GPU/expansion cards (unclip and unscrew)
  • 4Remove RAM modules (release tabs on both sides)
  • 5Remove storage devices (unscrew and slide out)
  • 6Remove case fans if required
  • 7Remove CPU cooler (twist pins or unscrew)
  • 8Remove CPU (lift lever, handle by edges, no bending pins)
  • 9Remove motherboard (unscrew standoffs, lift carefully)

Critical Reminders

  • Label all screws and keep organized by component
  • Never force components - check for missed screws or clips
  • Place parts on anti-static mat or labeled tray
  • Take photos throughout the process

Step 5: Cleaning & Maintenance

CPU cleaning and thermal paste application

Cleaning the CPU and Heatsink

  • 1Use isopropyl alcohol (90% or higher) and lint-free cloth
  • 2Gently wipe old thermal paste from CPU surface
  • 3Clean the heatsink contact surface
  • 4Allow both surfaces to dry completely

Applying Thermal Paste

  • 1Apply small pea-sized amount to center of CPU
  • 2Do not spread manually - heatsink will distribute it
  • 3Too much paste can cause mess and reduce effectiveness
  • 4Too little paste can cause overheating

Why Thermal Paste Matters

Thermal paste fills microscopic gaps between CPU and heatsink, ensuring efficient heat transfer. Without it, the CPU can overheat and throttle performance or shut down.

Step 6: Reassembly Process

Putting it all back together

Reassembly Order (Reverse of Disassembly)

  • 1Install motherboard onto standoffs (align I/O shield)
  • 2Install CPU (match alignment triangle, zero insertion force)
  • 3Apply thermal paste (pea-sized amount)
  • 4Attach CPU cooler (secure evenly, connect fan power)
  • 5Insert RAM into correct DIMM slots (listen for click)
  • 6Install storage devices (secure with screws)
  • 7Install GPU/expansion cards (seat firmly in PCIe slot)
  • 8Connect all power cables (24-pin, CPU 8-pin, GPU power, SATA power)
  • 9Connect SATA/data cables to motherboard and drives
  • 10Reconnect front panel connectors (power switch, USB, audio)
  • 11Manage cables (zip ties, no fan obstruction)
  • 12Reinstall case panels

Step 7: Cable Management

Proper cable routing and organization

Cable Management Best Practices

  • Plan the route: Identify where each cable needs to go before connecting
  • Use cable ties: Bundle cables together and secure to case
  • Avoid fan obstruction: Keep cables away from fan blades
  • Separate power and data: Keep SATA cables away from power cables when possible
  • Leave slack: Don't pull cables tight - allow for movement

Critical Connections

24-pin ATXMain motherboard power
CPU 8-pinDedicated CPU power (top left of board)
GPU Power6-pin or 8-pin PCIe power (if required)
SATA PowerPower for HDD/SSD/optical drive
SATA DataData connection to motherboard
Front PanelPower switch, reset, LEDs, USB, audio

Why Cable Management Matters

Good cable management improves airflow, reduces temperatures, makes troubleshooting easier, and prevents cables from being damaged by fans.

Step 8: Final Assembly

Completing the reassembly

Final Steps

  • 1Double-check all connections are secure
  • 2Verify CPU cooler fan is connected
  • 3Ensure no loose screws inside case
  • 4Reinstall case side panel(s)
  • 5Reconnect power cable and peripherals
  • 6Power on and check for POST (Power-On Self-Test)
  • 7Verify system boots to BIOS/OS

POST Check

What to look for: Fans spinning, no beep codes, display output, BIOS screen appears. If system doesn't boot, check connections, reseat RAM and GPU, verify CPU power is connected.

Cleanup

Reflection & Observations

What did you learn from this experience?

What did you see when you opened the computer?

Everything was packed much tighter than I expected. The small form factor case left almost no room to work — cables were routed flat against the chassis and components stacked around each other instead of spread out like in a full-size tower.

Did anything surprise you?

The RAM surprised me the most. I expected full-size DIMM sticks, but this board uses small form factor memory (SODIMM) — the shorter, laptop-style modules. Because the case is so compact, HP used laptop RAM and angled, low-profile DIMM slots to save vertical space. It showed me that "desktop" parts aren't always desktop-sized: the same memory that goes in a laptop can run a desktop when space is the main constraint.

Did you have any problems with the disassembly?

Working in the tight case was the hardest part. The SODIMM slots sat close to other components, so releasing the RAM clips took careful finger pressure rather than force. The CPU cooler also had to come out at an awkward angle because there wasn't clearance to lift it straight up.

Technical Knowledge Demonstrated

Key concepts and terminology

Component Functions

MotherboardCentral circuit board that connects and allows communication between all components
CPUCentral Processing Unit - the "brain" that executes instructions and performs calculations
RAMRandom Access Memory - temporary workspace for active programs and data
PSUPower Supply Unit - converts AC power from wall to DC power for components
Storage (HDD/SSD)Long-term data storage for OS, programs, and files
GPUGraphics Processing Unit - handles rendering images and video

Technical Terms Used

  • SATA: Serial ATA - interface for storage devices
  • PCIe: Peripheral Component Interconnect Express - expansion card slot
  • DIMM: Dual In-line Memory Module - RAM stick form factor
  • Thermal Paste: Heat-conductive compound between CPU and cooler
  • Standoffs: Screws that separate motherboard from case to prevent shorts
  • POST: Power-On Self-Test - diagnostic testing at startup
